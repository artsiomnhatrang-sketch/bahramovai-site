Instagram disabled my account: what to do in the first 24 hours

By Artsiom Bahram, independent consultant on Instagram and Facebook account restrictions · Updated November 9, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: First read the screen. If there is an Appeal / Disagree with decision button or a number of days left, the account can still come back: appeal once, in the app, and complete any check you're asked for. If there is no button and no deadline, the decision is usually final. Either way, don't create a new account and don't pay anyone who promises to get it back.

The first day after a disabled account is when most people make it worse: they create a backup account, send ten appeals, or pay a "recovery service" from an ad. This guide is the order I'd follow myself.

What exactly does your screen say?

Instagram uses different words for different situations, and each one has its own path. Find yours:

What you seeWhat it usually meansWhat to do
"We suspended your account" and a number of daysNot final yet. You have time to disagree, usually 180 days.Appeal in the app now. Suspension guide
"Your account has been disabled" with an Appeal / Request review buttonThe appeal window is still open.Appeal once and complete every check.
"Your account has been disabled", no button, no deadlineThe appeal was already reviewed, the time ran out, or there was no appeal option.See "What if there is no appeal button" below.
"Confirm it's you", a code, a video selfieAn identity check, not a penalty.Complete the check from your usual phone, VPN off.
"We restrict certain activity"A temporary action block, the account works.Stop for 24-48 hours, turn off automation.

Take screenshots of the screen and of any email. You'll need them for the appeal, and they're the only record of the wording and dates.

What should you do in the first hour?

  1. Check that the message is real. Instagram reports violations inside the app and by email from @mail.instagram.com - not in DMs. A DM from "Meta support" with a link and a 24-hour deadline is a scam.
  2. Log in from the phone you always use, on your home network, with any VPN turned off.
  3. Tap Appeal or Disagree with decision if you see it, and complete whatever comes next: a code from SMS or email, a video selfie, a photo of an ID.
  4. If there is a text field, write 2-3 calm sentences: whose account it is, what it's for, and that you believe the decision is a mistake. See what to write in an appeal.
  5. Turn off everything that could look automated: growth and auto-follow services, mass-DM tools, auto-replies that send links.

What should you not do?

  • Don't create a new account in the meantime. That counts as circumventing enforcement, and Meta links accounts by phone, contacts, content and Accounts Center. The new account can be disabled too, and it can pull other linked accounts with it.
  • Don't send the appeal again and again. One honest appeal with a completed check is better than five.
  • Don't give your password or login codes to anyone. Nobody outside Meta can restore an account, and real help never needs your password.
  • Don't reset your phone or delete Facebook "to break the link". Meta links accounts by many signals (devices, contacts, content, Accounts Center), so a reset doesn't break it, and deleting Facebook doesn't erase the links.

How long does the review take?

In the cases I've worked on, the answer came anywhere from a few minutes (after a video selfie) to a few weeks. It arrives in the app and by email, so check your inbox every day, including Spam. While you wait, don't switch phones and don't turn on a VPN: that can restart the check.

From practice: a suspicious login, not a ban

An owner couldn't log into her business account: Instagram had locked the account because of suspicious activity and asked her to confirm the account was hers. Instead of appealing in circles, she went through the identity confirmation path once. It took 9 days, and access was restored. If the screen mentions a suspicious login or a hack, it's account protection, not a penalty.

What if there is no appeal button?

No button and no deadline usually means Meta's decision is final in the app. The honest answer is that the chances are low, but check these first:

  • You live in the EU. Under the Digital Services Act you can take the decision to a certified out-of-court dispute settlement body, for example Appeals Centre Europe.
  • You have Meta Verified on a linked account (your Facebook or another Instagram). You can ask its support about the disabled account, but they often can't act on a different account.
  • The account had money, contracts or ads on it. Keep screenshots and payment records: you'll need them for ads support and, as a last resort, a lawyer.

FAQ

Can I get my disabled Instagram account back?

If the screen still shows an Appeal or Disagree with decision button or days left, yes, there is a chance: appeal once in the app and complete every check. If there is no button and no deadline, the decision is usually final.

From a few minutes after a video selfie to a few weeks. The answer comes in the app and by email, so check your inbox daily, including Spam.

Should I create a new account while I wait?

No. A new account during a suspension counts as circumventing enforcement, and Meta can disable it together with the old one and other linked accounts.

No. Instagram reports violations inside the app and by email from @mail.instagram.com - not in DMs. A DM with a link and a deadline is a scam.
